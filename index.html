Is a College Degree Worth It?

Exploring Education and Income Across America

🎓 The Education Equation

Does having more college graduates in a community correspond with higher household income?

This project explores the relationship between educational attainment and economic opportunity using publicly available data from the U.S. Census Bureau.

NoteResearch Hypothesis

Communities with higher percentages of college graduates will generally have higher median household incomes.

📊 Explore Real Census Data

The U.S. Census Bureau publishes information about education and household income through its American Community Survey.

The following official Census QuickFacts pages provide education and income statistics for three locations.

📈 What Are We Comparing?

Educational Attainment

The percentage of adults age 25 and older who have earned a bachelor’s degree or higher.

Household Income

Median household income, which represents the midpoint of household incomes in a community.

🔍 Why These Variables Matter

Education is often associated with greater employment opportunities and earning potential.

By comparing educational attainment and household income, we can investigate whether communities with more college graduates also tend to have higher incomes.

However, a relationship between these variables does not necessarily mean that education directly causes higher income.

🏛️ Why This Research Matters

Understanding the relationship between education and income can contribute to discussions about economic mobility, educational opportunity, and inequality.

🌎 Future Research

Future versions of this project could compare counties throughout Florida and states across the United States, using interactive graphs and maps.

Data Source: U.S. Census Bureau, QuickFacts and American Community Survey.

This is an exploratory project. No causal conclusions are drawn from the comparisons.
